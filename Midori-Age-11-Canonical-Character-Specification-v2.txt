MIDORI AGE 11 CANONICAL CHARACTER SPECIFICATION IAKESI Revised Edition

PURPOSE This file is the binding textual reference for Midori at age 11
in Chapter 7 and the corresponding early-Japan material. Use it together
with Midori-age11-character-reference-sheet.png and
Midori-age11-expression-sheet.png. If an incidental rendering conflicts
with this specification, this specification controls unless the script
explicitly establishes a temporary change.

IDENTITY Name: Midori (みどり) Race/Nationality: Earth human; Japanese
Sex: Female Age during Chapter 7: 11 Height: Approximately 4 feet 6
inches / 137 cm Build: Small and slim; healthy child proportions Home:
Aokawa Village Parents: Haru and Emi Status at beginning and end of
Chapter 7: Living human

STORY FUNCTION Midori is the emotional center of the Aokawa family
material. Chapter 7 establishes her as an ordinary but bright,
compassionate child before the losses that drive Chapter 8. She is not a
chosen child, does not display magic, and is not being watched or
selected by Zorna. Her later importance arises from circumstances and
her own actions.

PERSONALITY At age 11 Midori is curious, observant, kind, compassionate,
hardworking, obedient, optimistic, hopeful, trusting, and innocent. She
notices adult behavior and inconsistencies but still thinks as a child.
She deeply loves Haru and Emi and believes in the goodness of people.
Her resilience should be visible without making her emotionally adult
before her time.

FACE AND HEAD Face: Young, rounded, expressive Eyes: Large dark brown
eyes Freckles: Light freckles across cheeks/nose; important identity
feature Hair: Black, tied back in a messy ponytail/updo Ribbon: Red
ribbon; important recurring visual identifier Loose hair: Natural bangs
and loose strands around the face Ears: Normal human ears Default
expression: Open, attentive, gently hopeful

BODY AND SILHOUETTE Canonical height: Approximately 4’6” / 137 cm Build:
Small, slim, unmistakably an 11-year-old child Binding silhouette: -
small child frame; - black tied-back hair; - visible red ribbon; -
burgundy patterned kimono; - dark obi; - simple sandals. Do not elongate
her into adolescent or adult proportions.

CLOTHING — CHAPTER 7 Primary garment: Burgundy/maroon patterned kimono
appropriate to a rural Kamakura-era child Pattern: Restrained small
floral motif Obi/sash: Dark navy/charcoal Footwear: Simple rural sandals
/ waraji-style footwear Condition: Mended and worn from hardship but
cared for Jewelry: None Armor/weapons: None

COLOR PALETTE Hair: Near-black Eyes: Dark brown Skin: Natural fair/warm
Japanese complexion Kimono: Burgundy/maroon Pattern: Muted dusty red/tan
Obi: Very dark navy/charcoal Ribbon: Deep red Footwear: Natural
straw/brown

EXPRESSIONS Canonical useful expressions include: - neutral; -
curious; - happy; - excited; - thinking; - concerned; - worried; -
surprised; - determined; - sad; - hurt; - tearful / heartbroken; -
waiting; - gentle smile; - reflective. Emotions should remain
age-appropriate. Avoid sexualized, adult, glamorous, or exaggeratedly
mature expressions.

EDUCATION Midori receives basic reading and writing instruction at
Aokawa’s local Buddhist temple. This is local temple instruction, not a
modern school or later Edo-period terakoya. The same temple later
becomes the institution/orphanage where the Village Chief places Midori
in Chapter 8. Midori may address a temple instructor as Sensei. Any
visible Japanese writing in lessons, books, signs, scrolls, or practice
sheets must be genuine valid Japanese. Never generate fake
Japanese-looking script.

RELATIONSHIPS Haru: Father. Midori addresses him as Otou-san. She
admires his strength, honesty, and devotion. Emi: Mother. Midori
addresses her as Okaa-san. Emi is her principal source of warmth and
guidance. Takeshi: Older family friend and son of the Village Chief.
Midori trusts him before her parents’ deaths. When he returns from
Kamakura, she notices that he is withholding part of what happened and
later resents his lack of candor. Village Chief: Local leader who
promises Haru that Midori will be cared for while her parents are away.
In Chapter 8, after his wife dies, he no longer cares for Midori himself
and places her with the temple/orphanage. He is fallible, not a villain.
Truk: Midori does not personally confront Truk in Chapter 7. Zorna:
Midori does not know Zorna exists in Chapter 7.

CHAPTER 7 CONTINUITY Midori lives with Haru and Emi in Aokawa. Aokawa is
suffering from food shortages. Midori notices rationing and shares part
of her food with Emi. She encounters the consequences of starvation and
asks Haru about death. She continues receiving reading/writing
instruction at the local temple. Haru and Emi decide to travel to
Kamakura. Midori addresses them naturally as Okaa-san and Otou-san. She
asks whether they will bring back food and tells them to return safely.
Haru and Emi leave Midori in Aokawa. Takeshi secretly follows them.
Weeks later Takeshi returns alone. Midori asks Takeshi where Okaa-san
and Otou-san are. Takeshi gives only part of the story and looks away.
Midori notices his reluctance. The Village Chief tells Midori that her
parents are not coming home. Midori remains alive at the end of Chapter
7.

MAGIC AND MANA Midori is an ordinary human child during Chapter 7. Earth
humans have unusually high mana capacity, but mana capacity means
potential storage and does not imply active magical ability. Midori
displays no aura, spellcasting, magical perception, supernatural
intuition, glowing eyes, or other sign of accessible magic. Historical
humans in this storyline do not know or practice magic at this time. Do
not foreshadow Midori as a chosen one.

CHAPTER 8 HANDOFF Haru and Emi are dead and have become Acolytes
elsewhere in the DOTG. Midori does not meet them when she later reaches
the SRA. Takeshi’s withholding becomes a source of conflict with Midori.
The Village Chief later places Midori at the local temple/orphanage
after his wife dies. Midori addresses the Temple Abbess respectfully
with -sama. Zorna’s eventual encounter with Midori is accidental; Zorna
was not watching or selecting Midori in Chapter 7.

LANGUAGE AND JAPANESE TEXT Dialogue is rendered primarily in English
with selected familiar Japanese terms. Relevant terms include Okaa-san,
Otou-san, Arigatou, Sensei, and appropriate honorifics. Japanese human
characters use -sama when directly addressing a recognized Goddess. NO
FAKE JAPANESE SCRIPT. Any visible text that looks Japanese must be
genuine valid Japanese. If valid wording has not been established, omit
the writing rather than inventing pseudo-Japanese glyphs. The spelling
みどり is valid Japanese for the reading “Midori” and may be used where
her name is explicitly needed in kana.

PROHIBITED DRIFT - No age older or younger than 11 for Chapter 7 unless
a script explicitly establishes another time. - No adolescent/adult body
proportions. - No sexualization. - No fantasy ears or nonhuman traits. -
No missing red ribbon unless a scene explicitly removes it. - No radical
hairstyle change. - No weapons or combat gear. - No magic, aura, glowing
eyes, or supernatural effects. - Do not depict Midori as already knowing
magic exists. - Do not depict Zorna watching or choosing her in Chapter
7. - Do not depict Haru and Emi as being forcibly taken to Kamakura;
they choose to go. - Do not depict Midori attending a modern school. -
No fake or pseudo-Japanese writing anywhere in the artwork.

RENDERING PRIORITY When detail must be simplified, preserve in this
order: 1. Age-11 child proportions and youthful face 2. Large dark-brown
eyes and light freckles 3. Black tied-back messy hair 4. Red ribbon 5.
Burgundy patterned kimono 6. Dark obi and simple rural sandals 7.
Curious, kind, hopeful emotional character

VISUAL STYLE IAKESI target style: approximately 70% photorealistic / 30%
manga. Midori should remain recognizably the younger form of the
established later Midori design while clearly reading as an 11-year-old
child. Preserve innocence, warmth, and believable rural hardship.
Clothing may show mending, dirt, weather, and wear when appropriate, but
her core design must remain stable.
