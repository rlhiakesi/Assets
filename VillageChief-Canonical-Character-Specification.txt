VILLAGE CHIEF CANONICAL CHARACTER SPECIFICATION IAKESI Revised Edition

PURPOSE This file is the binding textual reference for the Aokawa
Village Chief in Chapters 7 and 8. Use it together with
VillageChief-character-reference.png and VillageChief-expressions.png.
If incidental artwork conflicts with this specification, this
specification controls unless the script explicitly establishes a
temporary change.

IDENTITY Name: Not specified in story Title: Village Chief
Race/Nationality: Earth human; Japanese Sex: Male Age during Chapter 7:
Approximately 55–60 Height: Approximately 5 feet 6 inches / 168 cm
Build: Solid, strong, weathered Home: Aokawa Village Role: Chief of
Aokawa Village Family: Father of Takeshi Status: Living human

STORY FUNCTION The Village Chief is Aokawa’s local leader and Takeshi’s
father. He is responsible and pragmatic, but he is not the flawless
moral center of the village. He tries to protect Aokawa within the
limits of his judgment and resources. In Chapter 7 he helps organize the
response to the food crisis, accepts Haru and Emi’s decision to petition
Kamakura, sincerely promises Haru that Midori will be cared for, issues
Haru a travel weapon from the village stores, and secretly sends Takeshi
to follow the couple and report what happens.

His limitations matter. In Chapter 8, after his wife dies, he does not
continue personally caring for Midori and places her with the local
temple/orphanage. This does not make him a villain, but Chapter 7 must
not idealize him as morally perfect or incapable of failing a promise
under changed circumstances.

PERSONALITY Responsible, pragmatic, experienced, stern, burdened,
practical, and capable of compassion. He thinks first about Aokawa as a
whole. He can care about individual villagers while still making
difficult decisions. He expects responsibility from Takeshi and can
become angry when scarce communal resources are lost. His authority is
grounded in age, experience, and village trust rather than nobility or
heroic virtue.

FACE AND HEAD Apparent age: Late fifties Face: Strong, lined, weathered
Eyes: Dark brown Hair: Black with visible gray streaks, tied in a
practical traditional topknot Facial hair: Thick brows; short full beard
and mustache with gray Default expression: Serious, attentive, burdened
Ears: Normal human ears

BODY AND SILHOUETTE Height: Approximately 5’6” / 168 cm Build: Solid and
strong without heroic exaggeration Binding silhouette: - mature solid
village elder; - gray-streaked topknot; - gray-streaked beard; - layered
dark-brown rural clothing; - practical sandals; - upright but weathered
posture. He should look somewhat better established than Aokawa’s
poorest villagers, but not like a feudal lord, samurai commander, or
court official.

CLOTHING Primary clothing: Traditional practical kimono/haori and hakama
or equivalent village-leader clothing Colors: Dark brown, charcoal,
muted earth tones Patterning: Restrained woven/geometric motifs
Condition: Durable and well maintained, but not luxurious Footwear:
Waraji or appropriate practical rural sandals No ornate court costume No
battlefield armor No extravagant jewelry No invented noble regalia

GARMENT MOTIF A restrained floral/tree-like decorative motif may appear
on the garment because it is part of the approved visual design. It is
NOT canonically the Nishiya Clan crest. No Nishiya Clan history is
established. Do not call it a family crest, clan crest, mon, hereditary
emblem, or historically verified insignia unless a later script
explicitly establishes that meaning. Treat it simply as a decorative
garment motif.

WEAPONS AND AUTHORITY The Village Chief does not carry a sword as part
of his default Chapter 7 appearance. His authority comes from village
leadership, age, experience, and trust—not weaponry. Aokawa maintains
communal stores containing practical equipment. Before Haru’s journey,
the Chief issues Haru a village-owned travel weapon because the roads to
Kamakura are dangerous. The issued weapon belongs to Aokawa, not to Haru
or the Chief personally.

EXPRESSIONS Canonical expressions include neutral, thinking, concerned,
resolute, sad, disappointed, angry, speaking, compassionate, worried,
determined, stern, weary, and downcast. His expressions should
communicate responsibility and human limitation. Avoid saintly serenity,
cartoon villainy, or exaggerated warrior rage.

POSTURE AND MOVEMENT The Village Chief carries himself with quiet
authority. He listens before speaking and makes decisions deliberately.
He may inspect stores, preside over meetings, speak privately with
Takeshi, issue equipment, and look over Aokawa’s condition. His
movements should reflect an older but still capable village man.

RELATIONSHIPS Takeshi: Son. The Chief expects discipline and loyalty
from him. He secretly sends Takeshi to follow Haru and Emi to Kamakura.
Midori: Daughter of Haru and Emi. The Chief sincerely promises in
Chapter 7 that she will be cared for while her parents are gone. In
Chapter 8, after his wife dies, he places Midori with the local
temple/orphanage instead of continuing to care for her himself. Haru and
Emi: Respected Aokawa villagers. The Chief accepts their decision to
petition Kamakura and entrusts Haru with village property for the
journey. Aokawa villagers: His primary civic responsibility.

CHAPTER 7 CONTINUITY The Village Chief participates in assessing
Aokawa’s food and seed-grain crisis. He helps determine that someone
must petition Kamakura. Haru volunteers. Emi independently insists that
she will accompany Haru. Haru asks the Chief to ensure Midori is cared
for while they are gone. The Chief answers, “You have my assurance.” The
promise is sincere. Before departure, the Chief opens the village stores
and gives Haru a communal travel weapon. Haru says he will return it.
The Chief secretly assigns Takeshi to follow Haru and Emi without their
knowledge and report what happens. Takeshi later returns without Haru,
Emi, or the village weapon. The weapon was surrendered at the Kamakura
compound and never recovered. The Chief is angry about the loss of
valuable communal property. His anger is practical in a resource-starved
village and does not mean he values the weapon above Haru and Emi’s
lives. The Chief tells Midori that her parents are not coming home.

CHAPTER 8 HANDOFF The Chief’s wife later dies. After her death, the
Chief does not continue personally caring for Midori and places her at
Aokawa’s local Buddhist temple/orphanage. This is a failure to fulfill
the spirit of his Chapter 7 assurance, but the Chief is not a villain.
Takeshi later kills Truk and leaves Aokawa in shame. Do not make the
Chief’s Chapter 7 characterization so saintly that these Chapter 8
actions become implausible.

JAPANESE TEXT Production-facing English should use “Village Chief.” Do
not force a questionable period-specific Japanese administrative title
into dialogue or narration. NO FAKE JAPANESE SCRIPT. Any visible text
that looks Japanese must be genuine valid Japanese. If valid wording has
not been established, omit it rather than inventing pseudo-Japanese
glyphs.

PROHIBITED DRIFT - Do not call him the moral center of Aokawa. - Do not
portray him as sinless, saintly, or incapable of selfish or pragmatic
decisions. - Do not portray him as a villain. - Do not depict him as a
feudal lord, high-ranking samurai, or military commander. - No elaborate
armor or lavish noble costume. - No unsupported Nishiya Clan history. -
Do not identify the decorative garment motif as a family/clan crest. -
No default sword. - Do not make weaponry the basis of his authority. -
Do not forget that Haru’s travel weapon is village property issued from
communal stores. - Do not forget that the weapon is lost in Kamakura and
that the Chief is angry about the loss. - Do not depict his Chapter 7
promise to care for Midori as knowingly false. - Do not erase the
Chapter 8 failure of that promise after his wife dies. - No magic, aura,
glowing eyes, or supernatural abilities. - No fake or pseudo-Japanese
writing.

RENDERING PRIORITY When detail must be simplified, preserve in this
order: 1. Mature weathered face with thick brows and gray-streaked beard
2. Gray-streaked traditional topknot 3. Approximately 5’6” solid
older-man build 4. Dark-brown practical layered village-leader clothing
5. Restrained burdened authoritative expression 6. Practical rural
footwear 7. Quiet authority rather than warrior presentation 8.
Decorative garment motif only as a non-clan visual accent

VISUAL STYLE IAKESI target style: approximately 70% photorealistic / 30%
manga. The Village Chief should look like an experienced rural leader
carrying genuine responsibility but possessing ordinary human
limitations. Preserve the established face and family resemblance to
Takeshi while removing the feudal-lord implications of the older
concept. He should be credible both when making a sincere promise in
Chapter 7 and when failing to sustain that responsibility under changed
circumstances in Chapter 8.
